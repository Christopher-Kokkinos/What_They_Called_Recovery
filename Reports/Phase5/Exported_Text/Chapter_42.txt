CHAPTER 42 — The Second Decoy

Friday began with five people knowing five different things.

That was the point.

Jackie knew when they were preparing to move her.

Voss knew one old contractor channel that still answered.

Vale knew one vehicle supplier that had appeared around recent movements.

Elias knew two places he could take Zoe if everything went wrong.

Zoe knew what Cain still cared enough about to look at.

Nobody knew all of it.

Not even Zoe.

She hated that most.

They sat in a roadside café outside Ludlow with a laminated menu between them and Zoe’s notebook open beside a plate of chips.

It was nine in the morning.

Elias looked at the chips.

“You know they serve breakfast.”

“This is breakfast.”

“It is chips.”

“Breakfast chips.”

He gave up.

Small victory.

At the top of the page Zoe had written:

SECOND DECOY Beneath it:

JACKIE IS NOT BAIT.

Then:

NOBODY GETS EVERYTHING.

She left the page alone.

No circles.

No arrows.

No pretending the plan became safer because she could draw it neatly.

The first decoy had failed for one simple reason.

Jackie had carried the whole real route in her head.

Cain had found Jackie.

Then he had found the thing she loved enough to break for.

Her siblings.

This time there would be no one person to break.

Jackie knew timing.

Voss knew his piece.

Vale knew hers.

Elias knew the fallback.

Zoe knew the signal.

None of them could surrender what they did not have.

Elias pointed at the notebook.

“You still dislike this.”

“I dislike not knowing things.”

“Yes.”

“I particularly dislike plans where I designed the not-knowing.”

“That seems fair.”

She ate another chip.

“What happens if Jackie moves early?”

“We adapt.”

“What happens if Voss gets nothing?”

“We adapt.”

“Vale?”

“Same.”

“If Cain ignores me?”

“Then Jackie still moves.”

Zoe nodded.

That mattered.

The plan did not require Cain to behave perfectly.

It only needed to make his world slightly less certain.

Her phone vibrated.

Nine twenty-eight.

Jackie.

Shift changed early. Bags downstairs. One driver I know, one I don’t. Cain not here. Legal woman here. Move likely before lunch.

Zoe read it twice.

The legal woman again.

Same person Jackie had seen at more than one property. That meant the move was not just a car. Paperwork was moving too. Authority. Or something pretending to be authority. A second message arrived. They took my shoes from hall. New coat on chair. Move definitely. Zoe stared at the last part. New coat. Not hers. Good. No disguise. No pretending to be Zoe. Jackie would leave as Jackie. The decoy existed somewhere else. Elias said: “Nothing back unless she asks.” Zoe nodded. Jackie already knew what they needed. Timing. Not heroics. Not registration plates if obtaining one meant staring too long. Not names she had to steal.

Not a location she could not safely establish.

Just:

I am moving.

Zoe did not need more.

“Unknown.”

* * *

Vale waited eleven minutes before touching hers.

The vehicle company was ordinary.

That mattered.

It had supplied anonymous hire vehicles on old jobs, but ordinary companies supplied ordinary vehicles to people who later did bad things with them.

Vale called and attempted a same-day booking.

White Transit.

Two hours.

Old contractor account.

The clerk checked.

“Thought it might be.”

“Do you have another?”

“No.”

“Then I can’t make the booking.”

Vale paused.

“No.”

Good answer.

Normal answer. She thanked the clerk and ended the call. Nothing happened. Ten minutes. Twenty. Then the old contractor portal changed. A line appeared.

TRANSIT — RESERVED — 11:45

Client hidden. Pickup branch not far from the region Jackie had been moved through. Vale stared at it. She had not booked the van. Maybe somebody else had. Maybe it had nothing to do with her call. Maybe the timing mattered. Maybe it did not. She photographed the screen. Sent one line:

VEHICLE MOVEMENT POSSIBLE 11:45. NO CAUSATION.

Then closed the portal. No following it. No driving to the branch. No trying to discover who had made the booking.

Her piece was done too.

* * *

Zoe’s part was harder.

Cain needed to see something that made him look away from Jackie.

Not a girl.

Not another coat.

Not a fake location full of people waiting to trap him.

Information.

Something real enough that Cain could not dismiss it.

Something incomplete enough that he had to make his own decision about what it meant.

The answer was already in the archive.

Her placement reference.

P06-11803.

Zoe hated using it.

Because it was hers.

Because it belonged to the room.

Helen.

Brian.

The glass.

That was also why Cain would care.

She wrote:

P06-11803 / SOURCE AUTHORITY CONTRADICTION CONFIRMED.

ORIGINAL PRESENTATION SITE. 13:00.

Elias read it.

Then read it again.

“You’re not claiming evidence is there.”

“No.”

“You’re not claiming anyone will be there.”

“No.”

“You’re giving him a real contradiction and a real place already connected to it.”

“Yes.”

“And letting him decide what thirteen hundred means.”

Zoe looked at him.

“That is the idea.”

“Good.”

The old family-assessment centre was closed.

No children.

No current staff waiting inside.

Nobody had to stand there pretending to be Zoe.

If Cain ignored the message, nothing happened there.

If he acted on it, he chose to.

Zoe looked at the wording again.

No fake documents.

No invented cache.

No fictional witness.

Just a true case reference.

A true authority contradiction.

A true historic site.

And a time.

The message went through a dormant legal-query route already known to forward restricted alerts.

Deliberately visible.

At ten forty, Zoe sent it.

Then Elias closed the notebook.

“One more thing before we move.”

“What?”

“The fallback.”

She looked at him.

“There are two?”

“Yes.”

“You know both.”

“Yes.”

“I know neither.”

“Currently.”

She narrowed her eyes.

“Is that you deciding, or me deciding?”

“Your decision.”

Better.

He continued.

“If the signal gets connected to us, there is less to extract from you if you don’t know which place I choose until we need it. But if you want both addresses now, you get both addresses now.”

Zoe thought.

She hated the answer she was about to give.

“Do I get to change my mind?”

“Yes.”

“If we move somewhere and I don’t like it?”

“We leave.”

“If I tell you I want the address before we arrive?”

“You get it.”

She sighed.

“Then don’t tell me yet.”

Elias nodded.

That was all.

No congratulations.

No pretending she had become wise.

Zoe pointed at him.

“I still hate it.”

“Expected.”

“Agency is irritating.”

“Often.”

“Don’t enjoy that.”

“I don’t.”

“You do internally.” “Unproven.” Some things survived. Good. They left. Not towards the observation centre. Away.

* * *

Cain received the message at ten forty-seven. Restricted legal called him. “Possible P06 exposure.” He stood before the woman finished. “What exposure?” She read the message.

P06-11803.

Source authority contradiction. Original presentation site. Thirteen hundred. Cain said nothing. He knew the site. Old Westmere family-assessment centre. Observation rooms. Recipient presentations.

One-way glass.

It should have been dead history.

The kind of location nobody revisited because too many things could be reconstructed if they did.

“Who sent it?”

“Unknown.”

“Who has seen it?”

“Legal triage.”

“Mac?”

“Not through us.”

Cain looked at the time.

Ten forty-seven.

The message could mean nothing.

It could mean someone had linked P06 to the site.

It could mean documents.

A witness.

A meeting.

An attempt to verify who still reacted.

That last possibility should have slowed him.

It did not.

“Do not forward it.”

The woman hesitated.

“Cain—”

“Hold it.”

“What are you doing?” “Checking.” He ended the call. He knew Jackie’s relocation was expected after the compromised phone. He did not know she was already preparing to leave. Not yet. He picked up his coat.

* * *

At ten fifty-eight, Jackie walked out of the house. No hood pulled low. No borrowed identity. No Zoe coat. Her own clothes. A new jacket somebody else had chosen. The legal woman carried a folder. The familiar driver opened the rear door of a dark people carrier. Jackie looked once down the street. Blue recycling bins. Woman with a buggy. White van parked half on the pavement. Nothing dramatic. No Voss. No Vale.

No Elias. Good. Nobody had come for her. She got in. The legal woman sat beside her. The vehicle pulled away. Jackie started counting. Left. Right. Roundabout. Long straight. Lights. Railway crossing. Not because she thought she could build a map from it later. Counting gave fear a job. The legal woman noticed. “You can stop doing that.”

“Doing what?”

“Counting.”

Jackie looked offended. “Maybe I’m counting sheep.”

“It’s ten in the morning.”

“Progressive sheep.”

The woman looked away. Jackie watched the folder instead. Not enough to lean. Not enough to get caught. Two tabs were visible.

RISK / INTERNAL CONTACT

MOVE AUTHORITY

She left them alone. Knowing when not to look was useful too. The woman’s phone vibrated. “Yes.” Pause. “No. We’re already mobile.” Another pause. Her expression changed. “That isn’t our route.” Jackie stared through the window. “Who sent it?” Pause. “P06?” Jackie kept her face blank. The woman listened. Then:

“Cain knows?” Pause. “Fine.” She ended the call. The driver checked the mirror. “What?” “Nothing. Stay on route.” Jackie looked outside. Something had happened. She did not know what. Good. That was the point.

* * *

At eleven twelve, Voss’s old phone lit.

AVAILABILITY REQUIRED — POSSIBLE 13:00 LEGACY RECOVERY

Vale leaned over. “They answered.” “They responded.” “That is answering.” Voss ignored her. A second message followed.

STANDBY ONLY. DO NOT CONTACT CLIENT.

Voss photographed the screen.

Powered the phone down.

No reply.

No attempt to discover location.

No attempt to assemble anybody.

Done.

For once, knowing when to stop felt more professional than knowing how to continue.

At eleven twenty, Vale’s portal changed again.

The Transit reservation moved branches.

Closer to the old presentation-site area.

She looked at the map.

Closed it.

VEHICLE RESERVATION SHIFTED TOWARD PRESENTATION-SITE

* * *

At eleven thirty-eight, Margaret called Mac.

“Possibly.”

“Unknown.”

“No.”

Of course he had not.

“Jackie?”

“Already mobile.”

Mac went still.

“When?”

“About forty minutes ago.”

Two separate facts collided.

Cain had turned his attention towards P06.

Jackie was being moved.

Contractor channels had woken.

Vehicle channels had moved.

Someone had introduced uncertainty into different compartments at the same time.

Mac saw the outline.

Not enough to prove who.

Enough to recognise the method.

“The first one used a person,” he said.

Margaret was quiet.

“This one doesn’t.”

“You think Zoe.”

“I think somebody learned.”

“Not the same thing.”

No admiration.

Only the uncomfortable recognition that the system was now being used against its own habits.

“Where is Jackie going?”

Margaret checked.

“Cain?”

“Not directly.”

Mac stared at nothing.

That used to be reassuring.

Now it sounded dangerous.

* * *

Jackie knew the route had changed because the driver objected.

“Instruction was north.” The legal woman said: “Instruction changed.” “By who?” “Central.” “That isn’t a person.” “Drive.” They left the dual carriageway. More buildings. More traffic. The legal woman made another call. “No, she’s with us.” Pause. “No.” Another pause. “What site?” Jackie looked at her hands. The woman listened. “That’s unrelated.” Pause. “Fine.” She ended the call. The driver glanced across.

“What?”

“They want authority verified before transfer.”

“To where?”

“Not our concern.”

Transfer. So this car had never been the final destination. Jackie filed that away. The move was no longer smooth. People were asking one another questions. That was probably what Zoe wanted. Not a rescue car roaring alongside them. Not Elias appearing with a gun. Hesitation. Jackie almost smiled. The legal woman noticed. “What?”

“Nothing.”

“You’re smiling.”

“I remembered something.”

“What?”

“Purple penguin.”

The woman stared. Jackie smiled properly.

“Never mind.”

* * *

At twelve oh three, they entered the rear yard of a small office building. A solicitor’s plaque faced the street. The legal woman went inside. Jackie stayed in the vehicle with the driver. Five minutes. Seven. Nine. The driver’s phone rang. “Yeah.”

Pause. “What do you mean, hold?”

He looked at Jackie in the mirror. “She’s in the vehicle.”

Pause. “No, legal went inside.”

Another pause. “Fine.”

He hung up. Jackie watched the yard. Camera over the gate. One side door.

No pedestrians.

She did not try anything.

The driver looked back.

“You alright?”

“Brilliant.”

He shrugged.

“Just asking.”

Maybe he knew what the move really was.

Maybe he knew only that he had been paid to drive a teenager under some kind of restricted case arrangement.

Jackie could not tell.

So she did not decide for him.

* * *

At twelve thirteen, a separate problem began elsewhere.

Another record now existed because somebody had reacted.

At twelve thirty-one, Cain arrived at the old family-assessment centre. One man with him. The reserved Transit arrived a few minutes later. Standby. No Zoe. No Voss. No Vale. No witness waiting by the gate. No secret archive being unloaded. Just a dead building behind fencing. Cain knew before he stepped out of the saloon. Bait. * * *

At twelve forty-five, the legal woman returned to Jackie’s vehicle. Angry now. Not panicked. The driver opened his door. “Where?”

* * *

At twelve fifty-eight, they entered a motorway service area.

Busy. Families. Lorries. Staff in bright uniforms. A school coach two bays away. The legal woman told Jackie: “Stay in the vehicle.” Jackie nodded. The driver and the woman got out and began arguing near the bonnet. Jackie watched them. The rear lock pin was up. Door unlocked. She could open it. Ten feet to the pavement. Twenty to the building. Run inside. Shout. Find security. Call police. Maybe she could get herself out of the car. That did not get her brother and sisters out of anything. It did not tell her whether Cain’s people still knew where they were. It did not tell her who would reach her first after she ran.

And it turned everything after the door into another guess. Jackie stayed seated. The choice hurt more because the door was open. Then she saw the coach. Children in uniform. Teacher counting them. Backpacks. Noise. For half a second her brain offered the old solution. Disappear into them. Confusion. Wrong child. Wrong direction. Zoe’s coat. The first decoy. No. Jackie stayed where she was. She was not turning another group of children into cover. Not again.
