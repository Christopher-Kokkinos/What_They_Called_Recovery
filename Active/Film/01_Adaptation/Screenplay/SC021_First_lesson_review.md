## Character turn

Zoe enters with refuge dependent on Elias's assurances. She checks her own room and exit, challenges his bounded choices, then discovers that his mistake brought the threat with them. Elias answers her accusation without an excuse. Trust develops through his admission of uncertainty and his reliance on what she actually sees. By the exit, she initiates checking the replacement car and they work together. Her competence contributes to their survival; it does not cancel her distrust.

Elias's old-life pain is carried by “Myself” and his turn to the kettle, without inventing a backstory reveal. His competence and fatigue coexist with a serious failure. Keep his praise specific to Zoe noticing behaviour and the mirror; avoid a general declaration that she is exceptional.

## Recommendations for production review

Estimated 7–9 minutes depending on pauses and montage; this is an editorial estimate, not a timed animatic. Preserve the accountability exchange and observation/payoff first if the full-film runtime requires a cut. The arrival banter and domestic transitions offer compression before removing the relationship turn.

Test whether the narrow road glimpse makes the driver's deliberate forward gaze and mirror glance legible in an animatic. Let the audience register ordinary traffic first. Distinguish a possible search pattern from certainty through Elias's language and performance.

Elias's initial boundary is intentionally controlling and Zoe challenges it. Do not play her resistance as childish or his authority as automatically earned. Her locked room, fallback route, independent report and shared sweep must remain visible.
