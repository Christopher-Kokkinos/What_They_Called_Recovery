# What They Called Recovery — whole-film treatment v0.1

Status: **approved narrative treatment** (author acceptance, 2026-09-27; DEC-0007). Grounded in the 46-chapter source map and decisions DEC-0001–0006. Approval establishes the whole-film narrative spine; it does not approve a scene list, runtime, dialogue, adaptation-register disposition or vertical slice. The novel publication master remains unchanged.

## Story premise and dramatic question

Thirteen-year-old Zoe Harper arrives at an airport under a false family arrangement with a backpack she refuses to surrender. Elias, a driver hired to deliver her to Mac's supposed mother, has spent years making a life out of doing assigned work without asking what sits behind it. The journey forces both of them to confront the cost of that habit. The film's question is whether Zoe can move from being transferred between adults, contractors and institutions to having meaningful authority over her own testimony, evidence, relationships and direction.

Elias is a co-lead, but Zoe's growing self agency anchors the major turns. Mac, Cain, the contracted hunters and later independent investigators appear in selective cutaways to show decisions and consequences Zoe cannot witness. The audience must always be able to distinguish what Zoe knows, what another character asserts and what an outside record establishes.

## Opening — a driver before the assignment

Elias wakes late to pigeons at his apartment window. His ribs and shoulder hurt; the unused prescribed medication in the drawer and a missed clinic call show recovery as an ongoing practice rather than a completed victory. He has written notice to Mac because recent jobs have demanded too much deliberate ignorance. He fixes Mrs Doran's door and carries her trolley despite his lateness. At his car he documents an erroneous clamp and removes its replaceable arm with methodical care. Traffic lights hold him in place while bills, pain and an oddly arranged emergency convoy compete for his attention. Mac's distinctive call redirects him to an airport pickup: the assigned driver is unavailable, and Elias is the person Mac claims is close enough and trustworthy enough.

These actions introduce care, pressure, capability and habit. Mac has not seen Elias's private morning, but later reveals that he valued a man who appeared reliably, handled complicated jobs and asked few questions. Elias pockets his notice rather than delivering it. The airport assignment becomes the last job he meant to accept and the first he can no longer treat as only a route.

## Zoe and the route that should have been safe

At arrivals, Zoe is guarded, exhausted and alert to every handover that fails to happen in public. She sets terms about distance, touch, the car and calls. Elias accepts specific boundaries but remains responsible for immediate road safety. She tests escape by starting his hatchback; he stops the vehicle without taking hold of her. Her fear cannot be explained by a difficult passenger label. On the motorway she notices a persistent car and a caller Elias will not answer while driving. At a busy service station, Cain's men close in. Elias sends her into a bounded three-minute wait with a public fallback and returns injured after quietly disabling the pursuers' vehicles. One man recognises something from Elias's past.

Zoe has reason to distrust his controlled violence, yet also sees him honour small promises. Rather than hand over her backpack or accept Mac's destination, she agrees to tell him the missing week on her terms. Their conversation frames the earlier events; the audience enters them through what she remembers and understood at the time.

## The missing seven days

The account begins on an ordinary Tuesday with school, Jackie and a short errand for milk. Zoe is seized from a familiar street and taken to a house where adults use administrative language, locked rooms, disrupted sleep and staged appearances to make children compliant. She notices a childhood photograph and a number in an older file without yet knowing how that file relates to her life. An older girl warns her about a drink before a showing; Zoe feigns compliance, then uses a dispute over records to take her own folder and a black drive and escape home.

Helen's relief is real. Brian's immediate concern about who followed Zoe is also real. They call Mac rather than create an independent police or medical record. Zoe overhears enough to question her adoption, while Mac directs an abrupt journey to his alleged mother's house. Jackie insists on seeing Zoe and helps make a decoy route. She takes Zoe's coat and leaves with a promised check-in that never arrives. Zoe concludes Jackie may have given away the real route. That conclusion is emotionally credible to her, not a fact the film confirms. The adults continue with the prearranged flight despite Zoe's warning. Only before departure does Zoe read the folder fields that connect her placement and Mac to an arrangement she never consented to. She boards as a tactical opening, carrying the folder and drive.

After this account, a brief separate Jackie cutaway shows the audience she is alive, watched and trying to protect her siblings. It withholds the precise distinction between the first route she was forced to reveal and the second she did not give. Zoe continues without that knowledge. The cutaway makes her isolation painful rather than making her judgment foolish.

## A choice, a lesson, a death

In the present journey Elias passes Mac's grandmother exit. At a public stop he offers Zoe a cottage and discusses other imperfect routes; she sets conditions before choosing it provisionally. He disables one phone channel, but Mac has concealed a tracker under the car. Mac hires Voss, Reyes and Vale under a recovery contract whose paperwork claims authority it may not possess. The team's skills and competing judgments make the pursuit dangerous without granting them full knowledge of Elias or Zoe.

At the cottage, Elias finds the tracker and admits the safety he offered was less certain than he said. Zoe's observation of a car checking the access road changes their plan. They leave in a second vehicle and attach the tracker to a detached trailer that travels east. At a farmhouse they build practical fallbacks, practice observation and gather supplies. In a market Zoe recognises Reyes and later Vale despite altered appearances. Elias trusts her sighting. The hunters begin revising their model: she is participating in decisions, not simply being carried away.

Reyes's pride and impatience become an exploitable weakness. Elias stages an old lock-up as an occupied refuge, using traces of his own history without planting Zoe's belongings. He tells her he intends a trap and rehearses what she must do if he does not return. Reyes finds the site and enters ahead of Voss and Vale. Elias kills him in the struggle and takes the operational phone. The violence is consequential, not triumphant. Zoe asks directly whether he planned it. He admits what he did, fears how familiar killing felt, and finally tells her about Sam, the younger man he taught and lost after an incomplete brief led them into an ambush. Zoe refuses to be treated as Sam's replacement or to have her learning limited by his guilt.

## Evidence instead of another transfer

Voss and Vale confront Mac over the false simplicity of his brief. Elias contacts them, and they meet Zoe in a public café. Both sides test the other's account, but a supposedly isolated contact route has passed through infrastructure Mac can see. As additional watchers appear, the hunters propose separating Zoe from Elias. Zoe recognises how that solution resembles every prior transfer made for her. She identifies an unplanned delivery-van exit; the meeting fractures under pressure, and she and Elias escape while Voss and Vale see that Mac's version of authority no longer deserves automatic trust.

In a city, Zoe directs a careful inspection of her drive on an offline machine. The records contain placements, monitoring, recoveries, overrides, household assessments and codes, but they are not self-explanatory proof that every listed case is criminal. She writes “probably Mac” beside AC019 and insists on proving it. Working copies are separated from the original. Zoe's questions become more exact: what is an ordinary process, what changed in selected cases, who could make the change and what external record can test it without exposing other children? Elias's role shifts from sole planner to partner in an inquiry she leads.

Voss and Vale check routes and old jobs from outside Zoe's drive. A disputed order shares genuine court details with an authenticated original but changes the authority and destination. Supplied verification contacts confirm the altered version; an independent court copy contradicts it. North Vale later confirms that a supplemental authority cannot be found and disputes the powers it claimed. Westmere's legitimate safeguarding work remains visible alongside a restricted channel that borrowed its forms and trust. Ordinary workers and loving recipient households are not treated as one uniform conspiracy. The fact that some records are real makes the selective misuse harder to see and easier to repeat.

Zoe examines Helen and Brian's household record and the matching process. Their grief over a lost child made them vulnerable to Mac's offer. They saw Zoe through glass before accepting her, recognised troubling signs and accepted anyway. The years of food, bicycles, birthdays and comfort that followed were also real. Zoe does not have to turn either truth into the other. She chooses when to look, when to stop and which private memories remain hers.

## The second decoy and the independent record

Jackie's first messages reach Voss and Vale, then Zoe. They establish that Jackie was forced to give the first route and withheld the second; her siblings were used against her. A private challenge response strengthens confidence that Jackie is the sender. Zoe wants to reach her, but Jackie rejects a rescue that could make the people holding her move the siblings beyond reach. Instead, the group distributes only parts of a second plan. A signal about the original presentation site draws Cain toward a place linked to the disputed authority while Jackie is being moved under that authority elsewhere.

Mac, now confronted by the accumulating evidence and his own responsibility, names himself to a duty safeguarding contact and reports the current transfer. An independent team, not Mac, decides what to do. The legal administrator stops moving Jackie on the disputed order. Jackie verifies the arriving workers' identities and asks for a solicitor and safe contact. Zoe receives an initially unconfirmed update and must endure the loss of control that comes with real independent intervention.

North Vale and Westmere preserve their own records. Voss and Vale sign bounded accounts of the old jobs and their reliance on supplied confirmations. Helen and Brian give their own accounts. Mac cooperates under counsel without immunity; Cain's channels fracture. Zoe speaks with Ruth so her wishes exist in a record independent of all the adults claiming to protect her. She does not release the entire archive. A narrow set of independently testable discrepancies, testimony and preserved logs moves the investigation beyond the shadow system's control. Later charges mark the beginning of accountability, not a solution for every child in the data.

## Ending — direction

Zoe hands over the original drive through a recorded, restricted evidence process while Ruth holds a separate envelope. The backpack that has carried her identity and danger is suddenly just a bag. She reunites with Jackie on terms both girls can accept and hears how her siblings' lives continued while she was held. Zoe considers separate letters from Helen and Brian without being pressed into forgiveness, return or permanent rejection. She asks to be recorded as Zoe. Elias receives help for pain and the dependence he fears repeating.

Elias prepares to leave because he thinks distance may protect her. Zoe makes him say whether he wants to leave, rather than deciding for her what she needs. They agree that immediate danger may require him to act, but afterward he must explain and they work out the rest. Their future is not solved by a promise. On a walk, the path divides. Elias gives his preference for the easier river route; Zoe wants the ridge and its view. He accepts her choice. They take the ridge together.

## Sequence-outline questions

- Test how much of the seven-day account needs depiction to make Zoe's later evidence and emotional choices legible within a feature runtime.
- Place the early Jackie cutaway so it creates audience suspense without settling the first-route/second-route distinction before Zoe learns it.
- Decide which external cutaways earn their place by changing suspense or explaining an otherwise invisible cause; avoid turning the restricted system into an omniscient monolith.
- Keep the institutional proof chain comprehensible: original order, altered supplement, circular verification, independent contradiction, preservation and limited disclosure.
- Build a sequence outline and then assign source-unit dispositions. No unit is implicitly omitted by this treatment's prose compression.
